🏆 Empirical Finding: Glicko-2 Wins at Forward Prediction (Filter vs. Smoother)

53 Consecutive Evaluation Months (2022–2026) • Zero Future Leakage

In this rigorous walk-forward test, models are evaluated strictly on unseen future games: for every calendar month from 2022 to mid-2026, ratings are frozen as of the prior month to forecast incoming matches before any results are observed.

🥇 Glicko-2: Superior Out-of-Sample Generalization

Glicko-2 acts as an online Bayesian filter with shrinkage ($g(\mathrm{RD})$). It dampens noisy winning streaks, preventing overconfidence. Consequently, it achieves lower Brier score (0.2140), 3x lower calibration error (1.16% ECE), and superior forecasting accuracy.

🔬 WHR: The "Boundary Penalty" at the Timeline Frontier

WHR is an optimal fixed-interval smoother. While unmatched for historical reconstruction, its unconstrained Newton updates cause endpoint volatility at $t_{\text{now}}$ (lacking future matches to anchor it). In forward prediction, it suffers from over-reacting to recent form (ECE 3.48%).

🧠 Layman's Guide: Understanding Brier Score (MSE) vs. Expected Calibration Error (ECE)

Statistical Reliability • Sharpness vs. Honesty Trade-off
🎯 Brier Score (MSE – "Match Sharpness & Decisiveness")

Measures how close each individual prediction is to the binary result (0 or 1): $\text{Brier} = \frac{1}{N}\sum(p_i - y_i)^2$. A naive coin-toss (50/50 on every game) yields $0.250$. Lower is better.

⚖️ Expected Calibration Error (ECE – "Probabilistic Honesty")

Measures whether the model's confidence percentages mean what they say across groups. If we look at all 1,000 matches where the model stated "70% favored win chance", do those favorites win exactly 70% of the time? Lower is better.

🔍 Why can ECE be lower while Brier is higher? (The "Cautious Honesty vs. Bold Decisiveness" Paradox)

A cautious model might cluster all predictions conservatively around 55%–62%. Its grouped bins match observed results almost flawlessly (low ECE ∼ 1.2%), but because it never dares to assign 85%+ to obvious skill mismatches, its individual squared error (higher Brier ∼ 0.222) suffers. WHR achieves the retrospective sweet spot by smoothing across all games.
